About time: A novel way of ageing Monotremes using an epigenetic clock

Abstract
Understanding the age structure and demographics of a population is critical for informing effective conservation and management actions. Some species are, however, difficult to age accurately, which is the case for monotremes as neither the platypus (Ornithorhynchus anatinus) nor short-beaked echidna (Tachyglossus aculeatus) can be aged beyond subadult stages. Epigenetic clocks have reduced ageing barriers and, utilising DNA methylation, provide tools with unprecedented accuracy. Using the DNA methylation profiles for 83 samples from known-aged platypus and short-beaked echidna (45 unique individuals), we assessed the Universal pan-mammalian epigenetic clocks to accurately predict monotreme ages and calibrated a monotreme-specific ageing clock, the Monotreme multi-tissue clock. We also assessed the effect of sample storage conditions (-20°C, -80°C and archived museum samples with freeze-thaw history) on the accuracy of age predictions to inform field sampling protocols. The Universal clocks showed low correlation between chronological and predicted age, and higher error when predicting the age of monotreme species. The Monotreme multi-tissue clock showed high correlation and accuracy in predicting age. We found no significant impact on predicted age accuracy in relation to storage conditions. The Monotreme multi-tissue clock presents a novel and accurate tool for ageing wild-born monotremes beyond ages previously possible. To illustrate the application to conservation management, we used this tool to predict the ages of 24 wild-born platypuses to inform management actions for a zoo-based breeding program and a small, semi-wild population.
